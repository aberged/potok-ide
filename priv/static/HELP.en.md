# How to use Potok

Everything you need to get going: how Potok works, how to organize your community in it and where to find things in the app. The pictures come from the made-up neighbourhood of Topolik and its neighbours.

## First steps {:#prvi-koraci}

### Getting in {:#ulazak}

For now, you join Potok by invitation. Someone who is already here enters your email, and you get a message with a link that is valid for seven days. Once you open it, the account is yours: set a password in the settings, or log in each time with a magic link that comes to your email and is valid for fifteen minutes.

On the log-in page you enter your email and password. If you turn on "Keep me logged in on this device", Potok remembers you for two weeks.

![Logging in with a password; the link below it logs in with a magic link](prijava)

### On your phone and your computer {:#telefon}

Potok runs in the browser, on a computer and on a phone, and there is an app for your phone too. You can also install it straight from the browser, and it then opens like an app. It is the same account everywhere, with the same groups and messages.

### Finding your way {:#snalazenje}

The top bar is always the same. The house leads to the home page and counts your unread messages. The bell leads to the notices of the groups you follow, # to tags, and the two icons right of it to invitations and requests. The hand counts the access requests you can approve and all that waits for you in your groups' processes. Your picture leads to your profiles.

The button with three lines opens the menu: every part of the app, your account settings, the language (Serbian in Latin or Cyrillic, or English), the light or dark theme, this help and what Potok costs. On a wide screen the menu stands on the left, with no need to open it. The two arrows at its top put it away when you need more room, and the three lines bring it back.

![The home page: groups, with their unread messages and newest message](pocetna) ![The menu with every part of the app](meni)

## How Potok works {:#kako-radi}

Potok is made of a few simple parts that fit inside one another. Once you know them, any community can be put together in it the way it really works.

### Account and profiles {:#nalog-i-profili}

Your account is you: the email and password you log in with. In groups, others see you as a profile, with a name and a picture. One account can have several profiles, say a personal one and one for an association, and you choose which one you are using.

A profile is unique or shared. A shared profile is used by several accounts together, so a building's council or a family speaks with one voice, while everyone logs in with their own account.

### Groups in a tree {:#grupe}

Everything happens in groups, and every group can have sub-groups of its own, as deep as needed. The home page is at the top, and under it are the groups you are in. A local community thus has a group for notices, a group for its assembly and a map of the neighbourhood, and the map has a group for each problem in the neighbourhood.

A one-to-one conversation is a group too, with just two members. A thread, a longer conversation opened from one message, is a group under the one where the message was written.

A group from deep in the tree can also stand in one more place, as a shortcut: among the sub-groups of another group, or right on the home page. A tap on the shortcut opens the group where it stands, as a shortcut does on a computer.

![Sub-groups as folders: the neighbourhood map open, with a group for each problem](podgrupe-folderi)

### What a group shares {:#sadrzaj}

In a group, people write messages, send files, plan events and start polls, and a group can have a description: a text, a page or a small app. All of it stays in the group, and those who may see the group see it.

### Membership and inheritance {:#clanstvo}

You get into a group by invitation, or by a request that someone approves. A sub-group can inherit members: whoever is a member of the group above is a member of it too, with the same roles. So the assembly and the neighbourhood map have all the local community's members at once, and whoever is no longer a member of the community is no longer theirs.

You can narrow inheriting down to roles: then only the members who hold one of the chosen roles in the group above come into the sub-group, with all their roles, and whoever loses the last of them leaves. That way the local community's council gets a group of its own without you inviting anyone separately.

### Roles and permissions {:#uloge-i-dozvole}

What each person may do in a group is set by roles. Every group has the role Admin, which may do everything, and the role Everyone, which every member has. You can add roles of your own too, say council or moderators. For each of the 20 permissions, a role says "Allow", "Deny" or "Not set", and a denial is stronger than an allowance.

A sub-group takes its roles from the group above. If it needs different ones, it gets a manifest of its own, its own list of roles and permissions. How what you may do is worked out from roles and a manifest, with an example you change yourself, is on the page [Access rights in Potok](/pomoc/prava-pristupa).

### Public, private and open {:#vidljivost}

A private group is seen only by its members. A public group is seen by everyone who is logged in, who can request access, and its name, picture and a short part of its description can be seen outside Potok too, by anyone who has the link.

A public group can also open its whole description to people without an account: Edit group, Access, "Description open to guests". Whoever opens its link then reads the description, and the alternative descriptions marked "Guests read it too", and an app in the description, such as Feed or Map, only reads for them: it shows the public groups below it, but does not write, vote or react. The chat, the members and who reacted stay behind the login, and search engines do not list the page.

![A public group as someone without an account sees it](gost-opis)

A public group can also open its chat: then everyone who is logged in reads the messages and writes, without joining. Such a visitor sends no files and opens no threads.

### Following and notices {:#pracenje-i-obavestenja}

You can follow a group you see even when you are not its member. Its notices then come to you: short news the group sends to its followers, apart from the messages and with their own counter. Your phone also gets notifications about messages, mentions, events and polls, if you turn them on.

### Apps in a group {:#aplikacije-ukratko}

A group's description doesn't have to be just text. It can be a small app, say a form, news or a map, which keeps its data in the group itself and keeps to the group's permissions. You get a few such apps ready-made when you create a group.

## Examples of organizing {:#primeri}

Three communities, each put together as a tree of groups. You don't have to copy any of them: take what suits you and change it as the community grows. Groups can always be rearranged, and roles added to. Each one also has a page of its own that shows, step by step, how to put it together.

### A local community's assembly {:#primer-mz}

The local community of Topolik is a public group with its area on the map, so even those who are not members yet can see it.

- **MZ Topolik**, a folder, public, with an area on the map
  - **Citizens' assembly**: a chat, polls and the assembly's event in the calendar
  - **Neighbourhood map**: a map on which each problem is a group with its own pin and chat
  - **Requests**: a form through which neighbours write to the council; each request gets a sub-group of its own, and the council decides in the form

The group has a manifest of its own and the role council, which may send notices to followers, publish events and manage polls. The assembly and the map inherit members, so nobody invites anyone to them separately.

A week looks like this: a neighbour puts the pothole in her street on the map, with a picture. The assembly talks about it, and the longer discussion moves to a thread. Before the assembly, the council starts a poll with a quorum and schedules the assembly as a public event at the community hall. Whoever follows the community gets a notice on their phone too.

[Step by step: how to put together a local community](/pomoc/mesna-zajednica)

![MZ Topolik: the assembly and the neighbourhood map](primer-mz) ![The neighbourhood map: problems as pins and a path along the canal](primer-mz-mapa)

### A co-op buying together {:#primer-zadruga}

- **Zadruga Livada**, a folder, private, with the pick-up place on the map
  - **Orders**: a form each member fills in once and changes until the deadline
  - **Arrangements**: a chat about picking up and swaps

What there is this week and until when to order, the co-op tells everyone who follows it in a notice. The pick-up is an event that repeats every Saturday, with a reminder. In the form, the organizer sees every order and a summary of them, and takes them away as a table. A dedicated app in the group's description can also run the buying rounds, the driver and the split of costs.

[Step by step: how to put together a co-op](/pomoc/zadruga)

### A building or a club {:#primer-zgrada}

For a small community, one chat group is enough.

- **Zgrada Cvetna 12**, a chat, private
  - **House council**: a chat for the council only

The chat holds the minutes and the house rules (all of them are also in the Files tab), a poll on the colour of the entrance and an event for cleaning the basement. The house council answers with a shared profile, so the residents know who writes on the council's behalf. A club, a choir or a sports team is put together the same way.

[Step by step: how to put together a building or a club](/pomoc/zgrada)

## Profiles {:#profili}

On the Profiles page you see your current and default profile and a list of all your profiles. Tapping a profile in the list changes the one you are using, and "Create profile" makes a new one.

A username has 2 to 16 letters, digits and the characters `_`, `.` and `-`. A profile picture is resized to 200 × 200, and files over 5 MB are rejected. A profile also has a description and public tags.

You share a shared profile with another profile: in the Sharing part you type its username. The invitation shows on the Profiles page while the invited profile is the current one, and once it is accepted, both accounts use the shared profile.

![Profiles: current, default and the shared profile savet-cvetna](profili) ![Editing a profile: name, picture, description, sharing and tags](profil-uredi)

## Account and deleting it {:#nalog}

Your account is your address and your sign-in, and your profiles are the faces you show in groups. In the account settings, the Account part links the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy), which says what Potok keeps about you, and has the **Delete account** button.

Before deleting you see what goes and what stays. The account goes, with its sign-ins and devices, your memberships, follows, reactions, tags and invitations. Your messages, files, polls and events stay in the groups under the name "Deleted user", with no name, picture or description of yours. A profile you share with other accounts stays theirs. Groups where you are the only member go with the account.

A group never loses its last admin: if you are the only admin of a group that has other people in it, first make someone else an admin. If you logged in a moment ago, the account is deleted at once, once you type your address. Otherwise a link that works for an hour comes to your address. You can also ask for the same link without logging in, on the [account deletion](/brisanje-naloga) page.

## Home and finding groups {:#pocetna}

The home page has two tabs: Conversations, with your one-to-one conversations, and Groups. Each group shows its unread messages, its newest message, its numbers of members and sub-groups, and its tags. Past 99 unread it says 99+. When you join a group you can read its earlier messages, but they do not count as unread: the ones that come after do. A lock means the group is private, and a globe that it is public. The groups you pinned stand at the top of the Groups tab, in a strip above the list, with the number of unread messages in everything below them, so one tap takes you to them.

Above the list are the filters: public groups you are not a member of, threads, groups you are a member of, created or follow, the kind of group and a search by name. The groups you follow stay on the list even when you hide public groups. The search finds a group even when you type without diacritics, in Latin or in Cyrillic. Groups can be shown as a list or as folders.

The home page also has My calendar, with the events of all your groups, the Map of groups and My polls.

![The filter by kind of group](pocetna-filteri)

## A group's page {:#grupa}

At the top of a group are its name, the path to it and the tabs: Messages, Files, Calendar, Polls, Notices, Sub-groups and Members. Polls appear with the first poll, and when there is no room for all of them, Notices are in the group's menu. On the right are the bell for following, the message search and the group's menu. When a decision or a form of some process waits for you in the group, a **Waiting for you** mark with a number stands beside them, and leads to the Processes tab.

![A group's header with its tabs](grupa-zaglavlje)

The group's menu has muting, pinning to the column of your groups, the group's description, sharing a public group's link, tags, notices, roles, creating a sub-group, inviting and editing the group. Which of these you see depends on your permissions.

The button with lines, on the left of the group's header, opens your groups as a tree of folders: the groups you are a member of, the public groups you follow and the groups on the way to them. On a phone or a tablet they open as a panel over the group, and on a wide screen they stand in a column left of it. The way to the group you are in is open, and the arrow beside a folder opens and closes it; the browser remembers which ones you opened. A closed folder shows the number of unread messages in everything below it, so you always know where to look. The field at the top searches your groups by name and shows the way to each one it finds. The groups you often go to you pin on top from the group's menu, **Pin to your groups**, up to 20, and direct conversations stand in a list of their own at the bottom. A tap takes you to another group without going back to the home page. On a wide screen the arrows at the top of the column hide it, and the same button with lines brings it back.

![Your groups on a phone: pinned groups, the tree of folders and the search](tvoje-grupe)

On the Sub-groups tab, the left arrow at the top left goes one level up: it opens the sub-groups of the group this one sits in, in the same view, as a list or as folders.

A group's description can be a text, a page from a template or an app. Under it are the reactions to the group and the group's place on the map.

![The group's menu](grupa-meni) ![A group's description, reactions and area on the map](o-grupi)

## Messages {:#poruke}

You write a message in the field at the bottom and send it with the arrow. On a phone, while you type, the top bars step aside, so that as many messages as possible show.

![A chat: a reply with a quote, a thread, reactions and a private tag](poruke)

- **Reply**: a message with a quote of what you answer. Tapping the quote jumps to the original message.
- **Thread**: a longer conversation about one message, so that it doesn't bury the main chat. Under the message it says how many unread messages the thread has, if you take part in it: you opened it, wrote in it, wrote the message it started from, or someone mentioned you in it. Only those who take part get its notifications.
- **Reactions**: one emoji on a message. Tapping a reaction adds or removes your own.
- **Mentions**: type @ and the name of a member or a role, and @everyone mentions everyone. The message stands out for whoever is mentioned, and they get a notification.
- **Editing and deleting**: you can edit and delete your own messages, and whoever has the permission deletes other people's too. An edited message says "edited" beside its time, and pressing and holding it shows when it was edited.
- **Links** get a preview, and long messages fold until you open them. A link to a form shows the form's title and subtitle to whoever may open it.

On a phone, press and hold a message: a sheet opens with quick reactions and every action. On a computer, the same actions appear when you move the mouse over a message.

![Pressing and holding a message](poruka-meni) ![A reply: the quote is above the field you write in](poruke-odgovor) ![A thread opened from a message](nit)

The + button next to the field adds files or starts a poll. One message carries up to 10 files, up to 100 MB each. Pictures, video and sound open right in the chat, and you can also drag files into the window. In the Android app, you send a GIF or a sticker straight from the keyboard.

If you start writing and leave the group, the text waits for you when you come back. This browser keeps the draft, for each profile and group, and the list of groups shows "Draft:" and your text in place of the latest message. When you send the message the draft goes, and when you log out they all go.

When you come back to a chat, a line shows where the unread messages begin, and with more than 99 it says 99+ new messages. The magnifier in the header searches the group's messages, in threads too, and even when you type without diacritics, in Latin or in Cyrillic.

![Files and a poll under the + button](prilozi) ![Searching messages](pretraga-poruka) ![A line shows where the unread messages begin](neprocitano)

## New groups and settings {:#nove-grupe}

You create a sub-group with the + button on the Sub-groups tab or from the group's menu. First you pick what kind of group you need:

- **Chat**: opens on its messages, and all members can write.
- **Folder**: keeps sub-groups together.
- **Feed**: news you swipe through, with a part where its editors write it.
- **Polls**: all of the group's polls in one place.
- **Form**: questions, answers and a summary of them.
- **Map**: sub-groups as pins on a map. It is not offered on the home page, only inside a group.
- **Ordering**: neighbours order together from a producer, in rounds, and one of them picks it all up and hands it out.
- **Page**: a text people see first, made from a template.

In the second step you enter the name and choose whether the group is public and whether it inherits members: all of them, or only those with the roles you choose. "Set everything up yourself" opens the full form. A new group starts out with its kind's picture, which you change in Edit group.

![What kind of group do you need?](kreiraj-podgrupu) ![Name, public or not, and inheriting members](kreiraj-korak)

### Shortcuts {:#precice}

At the bottom of the same choice is **Shortcut**. It makes no new group: it puts here a group that stands somewhere else, say a local community's citizens' assembly, on the home page. Without a search it offers the groups you are in, and a search by name, from the third letter on, finds every group you see. A tap on a group places the shortcut.

A shortcut has an arrow and the word Shortcut, and it shows the group itself: its newest message and its unread messages. The counters above it do not count them twice. Everyone who sees both the group it stands in and the original group sees the shortcut, so a private group stays hidden from those who are not in it. Whoever placed it and an admin of the group it stands in can remove it, from the row's menu, and the original group stays as it is.

![A shortcut on the home page leads to the assembly inside the local community](precica) ![Shortcut: the groups you are in, and a search by name](precica-izbor)

Edit group has everything else: name and picture, access and membership (public group, description open to guests, chat open to non-members, inheritance and the roles it goes by), what opens first, the description, alternative descriptions, the location and the danger zone for deleting. For a public group, you also see there how a link to it looks when it is shared outside Potok.

![Edit group: access and membership, with how the link looks outside Potok](uredi-grupu)

## Members and roles {:#clanovi}

The Members tab shows all members, their roles and who is online. From there, access requests are approved, roles given, and members removed and blocked. Inherited members are marked, and tapping a member opens a one-to-one conversation.

You can invite a profile by username, or someone by email: if they have no account yet, they get an invitation to Potok. Profiles are suggested from the third letter of the name on.

![Members and access requests](clanovi) ![Inviting into a group](pozovi)

In Roles you see whether the group has a manifest of its own or inherits one, you add roles of your own and, for each permission, choose "Allow", "Not set" or "Deny". The role Everyone can only allow.

![Roles and their permissions](uloge)

An app in a group can also have processes, which act in the group in place of people. They are at the bottom of the Members tab, under Processes, with the name the app gives them and their roles, and they are not counted among the members. In messages you know them by the mark Process, with the name of whoever set the process going, and in a sub-group a process made, its name leads to the app. Whoever edits the app sees there which permissions a process lacks, and an administrator gives them with a role.

How the processes are doing you see on the Processes tab, in the group's menu: next to each process, how many of its runs are going, waiting, done or failed. Whoever edits the app also sees where and why a run failed, and takes it up again or cancels it from there. At the bottom of the tab they also see the group's fields the processes keep, who wrote each last and when, and may delete them. Below those are the entities, with which an editor describes the data of the processes: an entity has a name and attributes, and each attribute a type, which is a text, a number, a yes or no, a date, a date and time, a choice from a list or another entity of the group. An attribute may also be a list, and may be required. An entity that another entity holds, or a variable of some process, cannot be deleted.

At the top of the tab, under Waiting for you, are the decisions that wait on one of your roles, from processes drawn in Potok: you see who sent the value and when, and the sub-group the process opened for it. You pick an outcome and, if you like, add a comment. The forms a process asks of you, or of your role, are there too: **Fill in** opens them. How much of this waits for you is also said in the group's header, from every other tab, and beside Processes in the group's menu. Outside the group the hand in the top bar counts it: on the **Requests** page, under the **Processes** tab, are all the forms and decisions that wait for you, by group, and a form opens from there at once. In the lists of groups an amber dot with a number shows where something waits for you, also in the groups below.

![The Processes tab: decisions that wait and the group's processes](procesi) ![The Waiting for you mark in a group's header](zgrada-ceka-tebe) ![Requests: the forms and decisions that wait for you, by group](zahtevi-procesi)

## Calendar {:#kalendar}

A group's calendar shows a month or a list of events, and it can include sub-groups. My calendar on the home page gathers the events of all your groups and, if you want, public events.

An event can be one-off, all-day or repeat every day, week, month or year. You give it a place in words, as a group that stands on the map, or draw it on the map as a point, an area or a route. With an event come reminders for members, an announcement in the chat and a notification if the time or place changes.

You answer an event with Going, Maybe or Not going, and see who is coming. "Add to my calendar" takes it into a Google, Apple or Outlook calendar. A public event is seen outside the group too.

![My calendar](kalendar) ![An event: time, place, who is coming and reminders](dogadjaj) ![A new event](dogadjaj-forma)

## Polls {:#glasanja}

You start a poll from the chat (the + button, then New poll) or on the Polls tab. You enter the question and answers, or pick the ready-made "Yes / No" and "For / Against / Abstain", and a deadline.

Under "More settings" are:

- one answer or several, and how many at most;
- a secret poll, changing your vote and answers added by members;
- a public poll, in which non-members vote too;
- who votes, who sees the results and who sees who voted for what;
- the decision rule (the most votes, more than half of the votes, two thirds of the votes or more than half of those who may vote) and a quorum.

![A new poll](glasanje-forma) ![A poll in the chat, after voting](glasanje-kartica)

You vote right from the chat. The Polls tab shows the open polls, those waiting for your vote and the finished ones, and their details show the turnout, who voted for what (if that may be seen), who has not voted and the results to download.

In a secret poll nobody, not an admin and not an app, sees who voted for what. The numbers don't show before the end, and a finished secret poll cannot be opened again.

![A poll's details: turnout and who voted for what](glasanje-detalji)

## Map and places {:#mapa}

The map of groups on the home page shows every group with a place that you may see. Groups close together gather into a circle with a number. You narrow it with the same filters as the list, plus time and pin, and its layers turn places, areas and routes on and off. On the map, the search by name starts at the third letter. Tapping a pin opens the group's card.

![The map of groups, with the local community's area](mapa-grupa) ![A group's card on the map](mapa-kartica)

You give a group its place in Edit group, under Location:

- **Point**: a tap on the map, or your location.
- **Area**: a border you draw with its corners, like a local community's, or bring from a GeoJSON file. The map then shows what lies in the area. When the borders of smaller areas show inside it, as local communities do in a municipality, its border gets a wide see-through band, so it shows even where theirs lie on it.
- **Route**: a walk, a race or a bike path, drawn or brought from a GPX, KML or GeoJSON file. The map also shows which areas it passes through.

For the pin you choose one of 33 icons or the group's picture, and the pin's corner shows the group's kind. A group with a map (Map) shows its sub-groups as pins, and "Add a place" makes a new one. An app in a description can take you to the Group map at a point it keeps itself, such as a producer or where goods are handed out: the pin then carries the group's picture, and the card the group's name and the app's words. The group's own place does not change.

![Location: the local community's area](lokacija-podrucje)

## Files and tags {:#fajlovi-i-tagovi}

The Files tab gathers all files from the group's messages, as a list or in folders of sub-groups, and from each file you can go to the message it came with. You find a file by its name, and the **Kind** button keeps only pictures, videos, sound, documents, spreadsheets, archives or other files. Potok remembers on that device whether you chose **List** or **Folders**. Only members download files, apart from pictures in a group's description and files from an open chat. A JPEG, PNG or WebP photo loses what it says about where and with what it was taken before it is sent, and the picture stays the same; HEIC and videos go as they are.

You put a tag on groups, profiles, events, messages and files, with the # button. A public tag is seen by everyone who sees the thing it is on, and a private one, with a lock, only by you. One thing carries up to 10 public and 10 private tags. On the Tags page you choose one or more tags and see everything that carries them. While you type a tag's name, the suggestions narrow from the third letter on.

![A group's files](fajlovi) ![Tags: private and public](tagovi)

## Following, notices and push {:#obavestenja}

The bell in a group's header is for following. One tap and you follow the group. After that, the bell opens the choices: push notifications for this group, the Notices tab and stopping following. The home page, one-to-one conversations and threads cannot be followed.

Whoever has the permission sends a notice to all followers on the Notices tab, up to 2000 characters. A link in a notice gets a preview as in a chat, and whoever may delete the notice can remove the preview. The Notices page (the bell in the top bar) has the notices you received and a list of the groups you follow. On the home page and on the map of groups, the filter "Groups I follow" leaves only them.

![Following a group](pracenje) ![Notices received](obavestenja)

You can also mute a group: in the group's menu tap **Mute** and pick for how long, an hour, 8 hours, a week or until you turn it on. Meanwhile it sends you no push notifications of new messages, polls, events or form responses, but a mention, a reply to your message, a reaction and a process asking you yourself still come. A muted group's unread count is grey and stays out of the number on the house, and a crossed-out speaker stands by its name. The threads of a muted group are muted with it. The menu then has **Turn notifications on**.

![Mute: for an hour, 8 hours, a week or until you turn it on](grupa-utisaj)

You turn on push notifications in your account settings, separately for each browser or phone. They come for new messages, mentions, replies and reactions, invitations and approved requests, events and reminders, polls and form responses. New messages of one group do not come one after another: after a notification, the next one from that group comes only once you have read the messages or five minutes have passed. A mention and a reply to your message always come. There is also a separate switch for the notices of the groups you follow.

## Apps in a group {:#aplikacije}

Some kinds of groups get a ready-made app:

- **Feed** is a row of news you swipe through, with reactions and comments. Its editors write them in the sub-group `.feed`, from templates, keep them as drafts and publish them. For people without an account to read the news too, turn on "Description open to guests" both in the Feed and in `.feed`: a new story takes that choice from `.feed`.
- **Form** has questions of many kinds, rules (who fills it in, once or more often, until when, anonymously), a summary of the answers, a quiz and attached files. In its settings, "When a response comes in", you switch on a process for every response: a post in the chat, a sub-group for the response and a decision by one role, with a reminder. Whoever holds that role decides right in the responses. For a process with more steps, [draw it](#nacrtaj-proces) in Potok.
- **Polls** shows all of the group's polls in one place.
- **Map** shows the sub-groups as pins, areas and routes.

![Feed: news from the neighbourhood](feed) ![The order form, as a member sees it](forma)

You can write any group's description from a template: news, photo, quote, event, list, link, notice, question or free text. A group can also have up to 20 alternative descriptions, each of which only certain roles may see, or visitors without an account too, once the group opens its description to them.

Whoever writes HTML can turn a description into an app of their own (Edit group, Description, "The description is an app (API)"). You then don't type the app into a field: you upload it as a ZIP with one HTML file, or as the HTML file itself, and download the current one as a ZIP. The file doesn't go to the server, only its HTML goes into the description. The same goes for alternative descriptions. Such an app can also run a process: when an answer comes in, it posts it in the chat, opens a sub-group for it and waits for someone with the role to decide, reminding them when they are late. Such a description can also hold a site from another address, in a frame: a site made for it follows the theme and the language you pick in Potok, opens where the reading stopped, and the group's address leads to that chapter. An app and a site can remember something for you alone, such as the last page you read; not even the editors see it, and you see and delete it in the account settings, under "What apps remember for you". What an app may do, and what it may not, is described in the [domain map](/domain#domain-apps). How an app is built and where each of its calls goes is drawn on the page [The architecture of a Potok app](/pomoc/arhitektura-aplikacije).

### Draw a process {:#nacrtaj-proces}

You can also draw a process without writing code, right in Potok, even in a group without an app. Whoever may edit the group may.

1. In the group's menu open **Processes**, tap **Draw a process** and type the process's name.
2. Under **When** pick what starts it, and set it on the drawing's start step.
3. Tap **+** on an arrow and pick a step: a message, a sub-group, a decision, a form, a push, a wait, a condition, a group field, setting a value, a call to another process, a signal or a wait for a signal. Tap a step or an arrow to change it: the text, the role that decides, the outcomes, the deadline, where the arrow leads. A sub-group the process makes can inherit the group's members, all of them or only those with the roles you choose.
4. Tap **Save**, then **Switch on**.

A process can start on:

- **a new value in an app**, say an answer to a form;
- **a message** in the group's chat holding one of the words you type, say "leak", or mentioning a role. Replies in threads and a process's own messages do not count;
- **a new sub-group**, say a place added on the map, and if you like only one with a place. Records and threads do not count;
- **a set time**: every day, every week on the days you pick or every month, at the hour you pick;
- **someone by hand**: those you allow get a **Start** button on the Processes tab, with a title and a text, or with a form of the attributes of an entity you pick;
- **another process** of the group, with the step **Call a process**: on the start step you say what the caller hands over, plain fields under **Fields it takes** or an entity of the group under **What it takes**;
- **a signal** a process of the group sends, with the fields or the entity the signal carries. With **Also hears the groups above and below**, a signal of a group above or below starts it too.

A step's text can put in what the process has, in double curly braces: `{{start.title}}` is the title of a run started by hand, where `start` is the start step. The names are offered under the text field. On a wide screen you drag the steps where you like them, and draw an arrow from the dot under a step to another step. A drawing that a run could not finish cannot be saved, and the bar above the drawing says what is missing. The app neither sees nor changes a drawn process, and its own process, if it has one, runs beside it. It sees only what of the drawn processes waits for its viewer, and opens a form and **Start** in Potok's own dialog. A change applies to the runs after it. The tutorials for the [co-op](/pomoc/zadruga#proces) and the [building](/pomoc/zgrada#kvar) show how this looks.

Those braces may also hold an expression: `{{group.kvarova + 1}}` counts, `{{upper(start.title)}}` writes in capitals, and `{{if(group.kvarova > 10, "many", "few")}}` picks one of two texts. A condition is one expression that is true or false, say `group.kvarova > 10 and start.title != ""`: you compare with `=`, `!=`, `<`, `>`, `<=` and `>=`, join with `and`, `or` and `not`, write a text between quotes and a number with a dot. The functions are `if`, `empty`, `length`, `contains`, `first`, `last`, `sum`, `join`, `text`, `number`, `round`, `today`, `now`, `lower`, `upper`, `trim` and `add_days`. Potok checks expressions as you draw: when a name is not there, or you add up a text, the bar above the drawing says what is wrong. A drawing made earlier Potok turns into this language by itself when you open it, so just look it over and save it.

A process can also keep **variables** of its own. When nothing on the drawing is picked, the **Data of the process** stands beside it, and on a phone the **Data** button opens it: what started the process, its variables, what its steps bring back, the group's fields and the group's entities, each with the name you read it by and the type of what it holds. Under **Variables** tap **Add a variable**, give it a name and a type, and if you like what it starts with. A variable can also hold an entity of the group, and you can define an entity right there, without leaving the drawing. The step **Set a value** gives a variable, or an attribute of the entity it holds, what an expression works out, say `ukupno + 1`. Every run has its own variables, so two runs never mix; what all the group's processes should see goes into a group field.

A variable can also hold a **list**: tick **A list** in its row. The step **Set a value** then picks, under **What it does**, whether it sets the whole list or adds an item to its end. The step **For each** goes through a list an item at a time: under **List it goes through** you pick the list, and under **Name of an item** you say how its steps read the item, such as `{{stavka}}`. Its steps are those on the arrow **for each**, and they lead back to it; after the last item the process goes on by the arrow **after the last**. How far it has come reads as `{{za_svaki.index}}` of `{{za_svaki.count}}`, where `za_svaki` is the name of the step on the drawing. It reads the list once, on its way in, and a list holds 200 items at most. Its steps do not lead to an end of the process: they always come back to **For each**, and they may wait for a decision or hold a **For each** of their own. Step by step, the [building](/pomoc/zgrada#obilazak) goes round its findings this way.

![For each: a decision fills the list, and the loop goes through it](zgrada-obilazak) ![Data of the process](zgrada-podaci)

When a process takes an entity, what it gets has types, and you read it as `{{start.sprat}}`. The step **Call a process** then asks for a value of its type for each attribute, and the required ones must be filled in: Potok checks that as you draw, and once more when the call is made. A start by hand with an entity gives a form with a field for each attribute: an entity it holds gets a group of its own, and a list a row for each item, with **Add** and × to remove one. An empty item is not sent. A signal is waited for with an entity: whoever waits says what it waits for, and a process that sends the signal must send it with that entity. If the entity changes later, the process runs on as it was saved, and its editors see on the Processes tab that the drawing should be looked over and saved again.

![Start with a list of items: a purchase for the cellar](zgrada-nabavka)

The steps after a decision read its outcome as `{{odluka.outcome}}`, and who decided as `{{odluka.by}}`, where `odluka` is the step's name on the drawing; these are offered under the text field too. In the same way the step **Call a process** starts another drawn process of the group and hands it fields. If you leave **Wait for it to end** on, the process waits until the one it called reaches an end, and the steps after the call read the name of that end on the drawing as `{{poziv.outcome}}`, so a condition can branch on it. A called process can also **hand an entity back**: on its start step under **What it hands back** you pick an entity of the group, and each of its ends fills in its attributes under **What it hands back to the process that called it**, as a call's fields. The steps after the call read them as `{{poziv.napomena}}`, each of its type, and the whole result also fits in a variable of that entity. A deadline says how long it waits at most: when it passes, it gives up and cancels the process it called. What a process writes starts no other process, so processes talk only when you draw them that way. Processes call each other at most three levels deep, and a process another one calls cannot be deleted. The [building](/pomoc/zgrada#majstor) shows how this looks.

![The Repairer's end hands a Termin back to the process that called it](zgrada-vraca)

The step **Form** asks a person, in the middle of a process, for what the process lacks. Under **What it asks for** you pick an entity, and under **Who fills it in** whoever started the process, or the people of a role. The form has a field for each attribute, a group of its own for an entity it holds and a row for each item of a list. Under **What it says first** you write the text above its fields, and under **Already filled in** what a field already holds, say `{{start.text}}`, the text of the message that started the process. Whoever fills it in sees that already written and may change it, and the form asks for a field you leave empty. For whoever started the process the form opens by itself, on whichever tab of the group they are, when it was they who just did what led to it: wrote the message, tapped **Start** or sent the form before it. If they close it with **Later**, or the form comes after a wait or someone else's decision, it waits for them on the Processes tab, under **Waiting for you**. A form of a role opens by itself for nobody: it waits for the people of that role in the same place, and one of them fills it in. The steps after a form read what was filled in an attribute at a time, as `{{form_1.sprat}}`, and who filled it in as `{{form_1.author}}`, where `form_1` is the step's name on the drawing; the whole answer also fits a variable that holds the same entity. With a deadline, the process goes on without an answer once it passes, by the arrow **deadline passed**. Nobody starts a process on a schedule, so its form asks a role. The [building](/pomoc/zgrada#forma) shows how a message with the word "kvar" opens a form.

![The form a process opens for whoever wrote the message](zgrada-forma)

A process that others call is **private**: only the processes of its own group call it. If you tick **Public process** on its start step and save the drawing, the processes of the groups above and below yours may call it too, but not those beside it. Their editors see it in the step **Call a process** with the name of its group, and what it takes under **Data of the process**. Such a run works in the group of the called process and gets only what the call hands it: it knows neither the message or sub-group the caller worked on nor who started it, and the caller learns which end it reached and, when the process hands an entity back, what that end hands back. If you delete a public process or make it private again, the calls from other groups fail, and their editors see on the Processes tab that the drawing should be looked over.

The step **Group field** keeps a value every process of the group reads: under **What it does** you pick whether it sets the field to a value, say the last fault reported, or adds a number to it, as a count. The other steps, in other processes too, read the field `kvarova` as `{{group.kvarova}}`, and a condition can branch on it, say when it passes 10. The [building](/pomoc/zgrada#brojac) counts its faults.

Every field of the group has a type: a text, a number, a yes or no, a date, a date and time, or a choice from a list. An editor declares fields on the Processes tab, under **Fields of the group** (a name, a type and, for a choice, its items), and a field a step sets that the group does not have yet comes with the drawing when you save it: a number when the step adds to it, a text otherwise. Only a number is added to, and a process refuses a value not of the field's type. A process reads a field as its type, so `group.kvarova + 1` counts, and a drawing reports the name of a field the group does not have as an error. The type changes while the value fits it, **Empty the field** clears the value, and a field a process uses cannot be deleted.

The step **Send a signal** tells all the group's processes that something happened, say that it is Saturday, with fields you type. A process that is on the step **Wait for a signal** with the same name just then goes on, and reads who sent the signal and what it carries as `{{prijem.from}}` and `{{prijem.mesto}}`. A signal also starts the processes whose trigger is that signal. A signal is not kept: a process that comes to the wait only later waits for the next one. With **Only from a process on the same value or sub-group** the wait takes only a signal sent about the same subject, say a payment for that very order. The [co-op](/pomoc/zadruga#subota) tells its buyers that way that it is pick-up day.

A signal stays in its group, except where the receiver asks for it: a wait or a trigger with **Also hears the groups above and below** takes the signals of the groups above and below its own too, but not of the branches beside. It takes what the signal carries as the entity it expects itself, by the names and types of the attributes, and a signal that does not fit does not reach it. A process started so has no subject or starter from the other group, only what the signal carries. The step **Send a signal** shows how many processes in the groups above and below hear it.

How processes, apps and a group work together, and where each of them stops, is drawn on the page [Potok, processes and apps](/pomoc/procesi-i-aplikacije).

### Sharing into Potok {:#deljenje}

You share a text, a link or a file from another app on your phone into Potok. You choose a group or a conversation, and what you shared waits for you in the field you write in. Potok installed from the browser does the same.

You share a link to a public group or a public event from the group's menu or from the event. Outside Potok, the name, picture and a short part of the description can be seen, and the whole description once the group opens it to visitors without an account. When you share a story from Feed or anything else from an app, the card shows just that, with the app's name and picture in its corner. The same goes for the page's address, which follows the story on screen.
